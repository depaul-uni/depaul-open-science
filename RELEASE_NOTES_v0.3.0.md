# v0.3.0 - Open Materials Chapter

**Release date:** June 5, 2026
**Source meeting:** SLC Meeting 8 (Friday, June 5, 2026)
**Lead author for this release:** Bradley Hoot

## Summary

This release adds Chapter 8 (Open Materials and Collaborative Protocols Sharing) to the Implementation Guide, presented by Brad Hoot at SLC Meeting 8. The chapter fills the last operational gap in the open-science lifecycle: the materials that produced the research in the first place. The stimuli a participant saw, the protocol a chemist ran, the transcription convention a linguist used, the codebook a criminologist applied.

## What's new

### Chapter 8 - Open Materials and Collaborative Protocols Sharing

A full chapter covering:

- **Why materials specifically:** the replication, efficiency, mandate, credit, and ethical arguments
- **The four-bucket taxonomy:** instruments and stimuli, protocols and procedures, conventions and standards, apparatus
- **A disciplinary tour** across chemistry, psychology, linguistics, criminology, computing, and library science
- **Where to share by material type:** general-purpose (OSF, Zenodo, Figshare, Dataverse), protocols (protocols.io, Bio-protocol, JoVE, STAR Protocols, Nature Protocols), stimuli (IRIS, PsychOpen, Open Test Archive), linguistics archives (LDC, TalkBank/CHILDES, ELAR, TLA, CLARIN, AILLA, PARADISEC, DELAMAN), reagents (Addgene, ATCC, Materials Project, CCDC), educational materials (OER Commons, MERLOT, OASIS), and hardware (HardwareX, OSHWA)
- **Linguistics deep dive** with the major archives, CHAT and ELAN transcription standards, the Tromso Recommendations on citing linguistic materials, and the CARE Principles for community ownership
- **Legal and ethical constraints:** IRB and consent, copyright on stimuli (with the "stimulus protocol" workaround), Material Transfer Agreements and UBMTA, FERPA, cultural sensitivity, and licensing
- **Collaborative-protocol consortia:** Many Labs, ManyBabies, Psychological Science Accelerator, Open Reaction Database, ManyPrimates
- **A seven-step sharing workflow** from "I should share this" to "it is shared"
- **Six discipline vignettes** showing what an open-materials practice looks like in each field
- **Validated foundational references** and a chapter-specific glossary

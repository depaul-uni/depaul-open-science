### **The Reproducibility Crisis: Evidence & Impact** *(25 minutes)*

**Discussion Starter** *(5 minutes)*: What does "reproducible research" mean in your discipline?

**Evidence Review** *(15 minutes)*:

- **Psychology**: Only 36% of 100 studies successfully replicated (Open Science Collaboration, 2015)
- **Biomedical Sciences**: 72% of researchers report reproducibility crisis, with pressure to publish as primary cause (2024 PLoS Biology survey)
- **Cross-disciplinary Impact**: Failed replications lead to wasted resources, delayed scientific progress, and in medical research, potential patient harm
- **Economic Costs**: Estimated $28 billion annually in the US alone on irreproducible preclinical research

**Interactive Activity** *(5 minutes)*:  sharing of personal experiences with reproduction challenges or failures

### **Core Open Science Principles: The FAIR Framework** *(25 minutes)*

**Overview**: The four foundational pillars

1. **Open Access**: Immediate, unrestricted access to research outputs
2. **Open Data & Materials**: FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable)
3. **Open Methodology**: Transparent, reproducible workflows and pre-registration
4. **Open Collaboration**: Inclusive participation and community engagement

> ### **📊 Research Practice Self-Assessment** *(Complete during meeting)*
>
> **Rate your current practices (1-5 scale, 1=Never, 5=Always):**
>
> **Pre-Registration & Planning**
>
> - I document research hypotheses and predictions before data collection
> - I specify detailed analysis plans in advance of data analysis
> - I use formal pre-registration platforms (OSF, AsPredicted, clinical trial registries)
> - I pre-register even exploratory or pilot studies when appropriate
>
> **Data Management & Sharing**
>
> - I follow FAIR data principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable)
> - I create comprehensive data documentation and metadata
> - I have institutional-compliant data preservation and sharing plans
> - I use version control and backup systems for all research data
>
> **Analysis & Reporting Transparency**
>
> - I use version control systems (Git/GitHub) for analysis code
> - I create fully reproducible analysis workflows
> - I report all conducted analyses, including non-significant results
> - I follow discipline-specific reporting guidelines (CONSORT, PRISMA, etc.)
>
> **Collaboration & Sharing**
>
> - I share research materials and protocols openly
> - I make publications available through open access channels
> - I collaborate using transparent, open platforms
> - I engage in peer review and open scientific discourse
>
> **Reflection Questions:**
>
> 1. Which practices represent the biggest opportunity for improvement in your field?
> 2. What institutional, technical, or resource barriers currently prevent implementation?
> 3. What specific support would be most valuable for your open science journey?

> **Main Resources:**
>
> 1. **Cobey, K. D., et al. (2024)**. [Biomedical researchers' perspectives on the reproducibility of research](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3002870). *PLOS Biology*, 22(11), e3002870.

>     - *Why this matters*: First global standard-setting instrument defining open science principles, values, and implementation strategies
>
> **Supplementary Resources:**
>
> 1. **Transparency and Openness Promotion (TOP) Guidelines 2025**. [Center for Open Science](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/top-guidelines).
>     - *Why this matters*: Updated framework with 7 research practices, verification standards, and implementation levels adopted by 5,000+ journals

>     - *Why this matters*: Systematic evaluation of intervention effectiveness with evidence-based recommendations for implementation

## 🔍 Discussion Topics & Activities

### **Discipline-Specific Implementation Challenges**

**Psychology**: Balancing participant privacy with data sharing; handling sensitive psychological measures

**Chemistry**: Sharing proprietary synthesis protocols; documenting negative results and failed experiments

**Computing**: Open-sourcing algorithms while protecting intellectual property; reproducible computational environments

**Criminology**: Navigating IRB constraints on sensitive data; maintaining participant anonymity in longitudinal studies

**Modern Languages**: Adapting quantitative open science practices to qualitative and interpretive research

### **Small Activity**

1. **Barrier Identification**: List top 3 implementation barriers in your field
2. **Solution Brainstorming**: Generate creative solutions and workarounds
3. **Resource Mapping**: Identify needed institutional support and training

### **Experience Sharing**

**Guided Reflection Prompt**:

"Describe a time when lack of transparency or reproducibility affected your research (as researcher or consumer). What was the impact, and what could have prevented it?"

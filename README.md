## About This Guide

The reproducibility crisis is real and cross-disciplinary. Funders, journals, and institutions increasingly require open-science practices, yet most working researchers receive no formal training in how to implement them. This guide closes that gap.

It is the year-end deliverable of the **2025-26 Scholarship Learning Community (SLC) on Open Science in Practice** at DePaul University. A year-long, monthly, cross-disciplinary community of faculty exploring how open-science practices can be implemented across research contexts. Every chapter is written by a working researcher with hands-on experience in the practice it describes, and every recommendation has been tested against real research workflows.

### Who this is for

- **Faculty and graduate students** new to open-science practices and wanting a discipline-aware starting point
- **Lab principal investigators** designing reproducible workflows for trainees
- **Research-administration staff** supporting open-science adoption at universities
- **Anyone** preparing a manuscript, grant, or thesis under a funder's open-science mandate

### What you'll find

A concrete, opinionated, link-rich playbook covering the full open-science lifecycle: pre-registration, open data, reproducible analysis, transparent reporting, open-access publishing, version control with Git/GitHub, open materials, and integration into a sustainable research practice. Each chapter pairs principles with discipline-specific examples drawn from chemistry, psychology, linguistics, criminology, computing, and library science.

## How to Read This Guide

- **As a book.** Chapters are self-contained but build on each other. A linear read takes about three hours.
- **As a reference.** Each chapter has practical implementation steps and links to deeper material.
- **As a website.** A rendered, searchable version is published at **[depaul-uni.github.io/depaul-open-science](https://depaul-uni.github.io/depaul-open-science)**.
- **As a citable artifact.** Every release is archived to Zenodo with a DOI. See the *Cite this repository* button at the top of the GitHub page.

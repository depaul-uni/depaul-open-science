# Reading List

Curated, opinionated, link-rich. Free to access unless otherwise noted. References below are deduplicated from across the chapters and grouped by category. The chapter source is shown in italics after each link.

Use this list as a one-stop bibliography for the SLC's year of work. The 'foundational' picks are the highest-leverage reads for someone new to each topic.

---

## Foundational - Read These First

If you only read five things across the whole guide, read these.

- [Bryan, J. (2018). Excuse me, do you have a moment to talk about version control?](https://peerj.com/preprints/3159/) *American Statistician*. Best read for non-coders on why version control is a research practice. *(Ch.7)*
- [Munafò, M. R., et al. (2017). A manifesto for reproducible science.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-016-0021) *Nature Human Behaviour*. The single most-cited modern statement of the open-science agenda. *(Ch.1)*
- [Wilkinson, M. D., et al. (2016). The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship.](https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201618) *Scientific Data*. The FAIR paper. *(Ch.3)*
- [Nosek, B. A., et al. (2018). The preregistration revolution.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1708274114) *PNAS*. The case for pre-registration. *(Ch.2)*
- [Open Science Collaboration (2015). Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science.](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aac4716) *Science*. The famous large-scale replication study. *(Ch.1)*

## Books and Free Web Books

Long-form references that go deeper than any single paper.

- [Pro Git (Chacon & Straub)](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2) - the definitive Git book, free online; chapters 1-2 are non-technical. *(Ch.7)*
- [The Turing Way](https://book.the-turing-way.org/) - open-source, ongoing handbook for reproducible, ethical, and collaborative research. The single best free reference book on these topics. *(Ch.1, Ch.7)*
- [Happy Git with R (Bryan)](https://happygitwithr.com) - R-flavored but the conceptual chapters apply to anyone using GUIs. *(Ch.7)*
- [Open Access (Suber, MIT Press)](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262517638/open-access/) - the canonical short book on the open-access movement, free open-access edition. *(Ch.6)*
- [R for Data Science (Wickham, Çetinkaya-Rundel & Grolemund)](https://r4ds.hadley.nz) - free and accessible. *(Ch.4)*
- [Research Software Engineering with Python](https://merely-useful.tech/py-rse/) - accessible chapters on GitHub-based collaboration. *(Ch.4, Ch.7)*
- [Big Data, Little Data, No Data (Borgman)](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262529914/big-data-little-data-no-data/) - on data sharing and scholarly infrastructure. *(Ch.3)*
